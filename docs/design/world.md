# World, catastrophe, regions and systemic narrative

## World premise

A maritime world is undergoing accelerating freeze. Oceans remain partly open amid pack ice, floes, dead channels, islands, wrecks, abandoned ports, industrial facilities and occasional isolated shelters. Temperature, wind and storms, ice pressure and depletion drive ongoing movement. Distant zones may become more dangerous and sometimes more rewarding. The underlying catastrophe is **not initially explained**, perhaps never reduced to a single answer.

An in-world loose term (e.g. *the Haze / the Pale / the Drift*) may emerge, but **no name or cause has been canonized**. The same observations may fit volcanic aerosol, biological catastrophe, fallout, geoengineering, or multiple interacting causes.

## Zone progression (working taxonomy)

Coastal Freeze → Pack Ice → Deep Freeze → Polar Dead Zone. These are climatic/navigation profiles, not a linear quest order. Data determines sea/air temperature, storms, currents, ice distribution, resource density, human activity, contamination, sight distance and entry preparation thresholds.

## Simulated actors

**Accepted scarcity:** very few humans survive in the outside world. Rare isolated individuals may be found, rescued and recruited onto the player's ship. Small raider bands or occasional human contacts may exist, but dense populations, thriving settlement networks and off-screen settlement economies are **not an approved gameplay system**. Island, wreck and lab sites are primarily bounded excursions for materials, dangers, traces of the past and uncommon encounters. Time-dependent events need only be simulated as far as they create credible, consequential changes for the voyage; no autonomous city-state or exhaustive background NPC physics. Rumors, radio chatter and aftermath remain partial observations, not perfect truth.

## Three threat families

- **Nature:** dynamic storms, progressive freeze, disease, starvation and loss of heat.
- **Humans:** rare stranded people, salvage competition, isolated hostile groups and raiders with improvised boats. The presence of small factions does not imply an economy/population simulator.
- **Unknown:** mutated marine/polar life, fungal colonies, engineered specimens, contaminated industrial/laboratory zones, intentional experiments, unexplained anomalies.

Avoid turning every unknown into a generic zombie. Environment tells history through placements, logs, wrecks, scavenged machines and inconsistent accounts.

## Mission and story framework

Rare distress calls, rescue decisions, personal crew histories, occasional trade/hostility and rumors can emerge from authored content and lightweight, ship-relevant simulation. A rumored warm current / convoy / reactor / research facility can become a long-term objective without enforcing a mandatory path. Multiple options may include assist, steal, ignore, negotiate, expose or return later; world state continues between visits.

## System contracts

Each visitable region/site defines identity, seed, environmental profile, access constraints, discoverable content, persistent diffs and exposure/resolution states. **Persistence does not require independent simulated inhabitants or an evolving settlement at every island.** Events must identify provenance and time. Procedural generation should produce navigable and tactically readable play spaces, with hand-authored anchors where needed. Seeds are not a replacement for save-state changes.

## Deferred design decisions

Exact weather/ice physics, map scale, region load strategy, world threat progression curve, any optional bounded faction/encounter behavior and catastrophic explanation remain experiments/decisions, never implied implementation commitments. **Ship-first focus, sparse humanity and 12-person maximum onboard are accepted constraints**, not open questions.
